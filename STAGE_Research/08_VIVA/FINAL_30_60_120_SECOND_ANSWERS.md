# 30 / 60 / 120 Second Answers

## A. 30 SECONDS
I analyzed MoRTH road-accident data for 2018-2024, 254 state-years, across geography, time, weather and severity. Fatalities rose faster than accidents; weather shows real but non-causal associations; tested ML models could not beat a naive baseline, which is itself the finding. The contribution is a validated, reusable analytics platform with all limits documented.

## B. 60 SECONDS
My project is a multi-level analysis of Indian road accidents using verified MoRTH data from 2018 to 2024, 254 state-years across 38 entities. Methods run from India, zone and state trends through weather correlations with FDR correction to supervised prediction tests. Key results: severity rose from 33.50 to 36.33, zones differ detectably, rainfall and temperature associate with counts but never causally, and the naive baseline beat every trained model on held-out years. The live platform packages all of this with full provenance and honest unavailable-data handling.

## C. 120 SECONDS
India records nearly five lakh road accidents yearly, but totals alone explain nothing about geography, timing, environment or severity. I built a validated framework on the MoRTH 2018-2024 benchmark, 254 state-years, keeping NCRB monthly data, IMD weather with 236 matches, and seven supporting modules as separate vintages. Findings: fatalities up 12.4% against 3.7% accident growth; Friedman confirms zone differences; weather correlates but never causes; overspeeding dominates reported categories without proving causation; two-wheelers hold the largest death share without implying highest risk. Prediction testing is the honest core: on 2023-2024 held-out data the naive baseline beat all five trained models, and K-means gave two descriptive profiles. The platform is live and reusable, and every limit, from missing vehicle exposure to absent microdata, is documented rather than estimated.
