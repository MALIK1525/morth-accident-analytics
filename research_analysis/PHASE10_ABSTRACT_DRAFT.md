# Abstract (draft; verified numbers only)

Background: Indian road-traffic accidents vary across states, years, and factors, but exposure
denominators and microdata are scarce. Objective: relate geographical, temporal, environmental,
road-user, violation, and collision factors to accident occurrence and severity using verified
sources. Methods: MoRTH 2018-2024 state panel (254 obs) with vintage control; AR2017 2014-2017
candidate; NCRB ADSI 2024 monthly (separate vintage); IMD rain/temp aggregated to states (236
matched); AR 2024 supporting tables; OLS/corr/chi2/Friedman with BH-FDR; lag-only ML with
chronological holds and naive baseline; K-means typology. Results: accidents +3.7% vs fatalities
+12.4% (severity 33.5 -> 36.3/100 acc); pooled rain r=-0.23/-0.38, Tmax r=+0.45/+0.50 (associational);
no weather-severity link; two-wheelers 46.2% of deaths by count; no trained model beat persistence
(acc RMSE 1038.6 vs 2148.6 best trained); K=2 descriptive profiles. Conclusion: severity rises while
counts fluctuate; exposure-free risk ranking, causal, and forecasting claims are not supported;
state vehicle stock and microdata are the binding gaps. Keywords: road safety India; MoRTH;
severity; weather association; persistence baseline; K-means typology.
